SCOPE & APPLICATION
This procedure applies to all personnel authorized to operate PUMP-XYZ, including:
  - Shift supervisors and operators
  - Maintenance technicians performing system commissioning
  - Engineering personnel conducting tests or startups after maintenance

Pre-requisites for Authorization:
  - Completion of 4-hour pump operation training module (certification valid 12 months)
  - Hands-on shadowing with authorized operator (minimum 2 startups observed)
  - Written test: 80% passing score on pump systems and safety (valid 6 months)

SAFETY WARNINGS & HAZARDS
** CRITICAL HAZARD: ROTATING EQUIPMENT - ENTANGLEMENT RISK **
  - KEEP ALL BODY PARTS, LOOSE CLOTHING, AND TOOLS AWAY FROM PUMP COUPLING
  - Minimum 1-meter clearance from rotating shaft during operation
  - Do NOT remove coupling guard during operation
  - Do NOT attempt to stop pump by hand or objects (use stop command only)

** HYDRAULIC PRESSURE HAZARD: 25 BAR (362 PSI) SYSTEM **
  - Pressurized fluid can cause severe injection injuries (penetrating wounds)
  - Never point discharge hose at body or other personnel
  - Always wear high-pressure-resistant gloves (Kevlar-lined) when handling hoses
  - Pressure relief valve set to 32 bar (maximum safe limit) - do NOT tamper

** THERMAL HAZARD: HOT FLUID (55-65°C OPERATING) **
  - Hydraulic fluid can reach 65°C during operation (steam generation risk)
  - Avoid contact with discharge hose and return lines
  - Wear safety glasses to protect from possible fluid spray
  - Allow 30 minutes cool-down time before maintenance work

** ELECTRICAL HAZARD: 3-PHASE, 50 Hz, 6.5 A MOTOR **
  - Pump motor is connected to main electrical supply (220V, 3-phase)
  - LOCKOUT/TAGOUT required before maintenance work
  - Do NOT operate pump with wet hands or in rain/wet environment
  - Motor circuit breaker must be accessible (clear of obstruction)

** NOISE HAZARD: 76 dB(A) SOUND LEVEL **
  - Prolonged exposure without hearing protection can cause hearing loss
  - Wear foam ear plugs or over-ear earmuffs if operating >30 minutes continuously
  - Report abnormal noise (grinding, cavitation) to supervisor immediately

** CAVITATION RISK: INLET BLOCKAGE OR LOW SUCTION PRESSURE **
  - Cavitation can develop within 30-60 minutes of low inlet pressure (<1.0 bar)
  - Audible symptom: "Grinding" or "crackling" sound from pump body
  - DO NOT ignore cavitation noise - immediate shutdown required
  - Post-incident from January 2024: Enhanced monitoring procedures implemented

EMERGENCY STOP PROCEDURE (Do this immediately if any hazard observed):
  1. Press RED emergency stop button on control panel (large red mushroom button)
  2. Do NOT attempt manual shutdown using hand wheel
  3. Wait 3 minutes for pump to coast to complete stop
  4. If pump continues running after red button press: 
     - Close main electrical breaker (second lever on panel)
     - Activate system shutdown relay: Press STOP and hold for 5 seconds
  5. Call maintenance supervisor (Ext. 4521) for fault diagnosis
  6. Do NOT restart without supervisor authorization

STARTUP SEQUENCE - STEP BY STEP INSTRUCTIONS
================================================================================

PRE-STARTUP CHECKLIST (Perform 15 minutes before startup)
Equipment Location: Unit A Pump Room
Time Allocated: 15 minutes
Personnel: Authorized operator (minimum 1 person)

□ STEP 1: VISUAL INSPECTION - External Equipment
  Time: 2 minutes
  Actions:
    1. Check pump body for visible leaks (inspect all seal drain ports)
       Expected: Dry condition. Acceptable: <1 drop per hour from drain line
       Action if failed: Stop. Report to maintenance supervisor.
    2. Verify coupling guard is firmly in place and undamaged
       Inspection point: 4 bolts holding guard (hand-check tightness)
    3. Check inlet/outlet hose connections for tight couplings
       Test: Hand-pull each hose gently (should not move >2 mm)
    4. Verify pump foundation bolts tight (visual inspection of bolt heads)
       Method: Visually inspect all 4 foundation bolts for tightness
    5. Check for loose tools or objects within 1-meter radius of pump
       Action: Remove any tools or debris before proceeding

□ STEP 2: RESERVOIR FLUID LEVEL CHECK
  Time: 3 minutes
  Location: Hydraulic fluid reservoir (250-liter tank, northeast corner of Unit A)
  Actions:
    1. Locate transparent fluid level gauge on side of reservoir
    2. Check fluid level: Should be between MIN and MAX marks on gauge
       Normal range: Between 100 and 200 liters (shown on gauge scale)
       RED ZONE: Below 80 liters (suction starvation risk)
    3. If level is LOW (below MIN mark):
       - Stop startup - suction pressure will be insufficient
       - Add ISO VG 32 hydraulic fluid to MAX mark
       - Use proper filtering (40-micron funnel) during refill
       - Estimated fill time: 5 minutes per 10 liters
    4. Verify fluid condition visually: Fluid should be clear to light amber
       Black or dark brown color indicates oxidation - STOP and notify maintenance
    5. Check reservoir vent filter is NOT clogged
       Method: Visual inspection of vent screen on top of tank
       If clogged: Replace vent filter (takes 1 minute)

□ STEP 3: CONTROL PANEL INSPECTION
  Time: 2 minutes
  Location: Pump control panel (mounted on Unit A wall, left side)
  Actions:
    1. Verify main electrical breaker is in OFF position (lever down)
    2. Check emergency stop (red button) is in RESET position (button out)
       If red button is IN (depressed): Press and twist counterclockwise to RESET
    3. Verify all warning lights are OFF (no red/amber lights illuminated)
    4. Check that mode selector switch is in STOP position (lever left)
    5. Confirm that pressure gauge needle is at zero (0 bar)

□ STEP 4: SYSTEM STARTUP CHECKLIST
  Time: 1 minute
  Actions:
    1. Ensure all personnel are at least 2 meters away from pump
    2. Confirm pump room door is closed and posted with "EQUIPMENT RUNNING" sign
    3. Verify that discharge isolation ball valve is OPEN
       Location: Discharge line, 0.5 meters from pump outlet
       Action: Turn handle parallel to pipe (OPEN position)
    4. Verify that inlet isolation ball valve is OPEN
       Location: Inlet line, 1.0 meters from pump inlet
       Action: Turn handle parallel to pipe (OPEN position)
    5. Check that pump oil cooler thermostat is functioning
       Observation: Cooler fan should start automatically when fluid >54°C
    6. Final confirmation: All personnel clear from pump area? YES / NO

STARTUP SEQUENCE - PRESSURE RAMP (DO NOT SKIP STEPS)

□ STEP 5: PUMP MOTOR ENERGIZATION
  Time: 1 minute
  Instructions:
    1. Stand in front of control panel, facing emergency stop button
    2. Turn main electrical breaker to ON (lever up)
       Signal: Green light on panel illuminates (power available)
    3. Wait 3 seconds (allowing motor capacitors to charge)
    4. Press green START button (large green button on control panel)
    5. Listen for motor hum (3-phase motor start sound)
       Duration: 2-3 seconds (ramp-up phase)
       Expected: Pump begins rotating with audible mechanical sound
    6. Confirm motor reaches full 1450 RPM speed (no unusual noise)
       Timing: Should reach full speed within 5 seconds of start button press

□ STEP 6: PRESSURE BUILDUP PHASE (GRADUAL - No sudden jumps)
  Time: 2 minutes
  
  ** IMPORTANT: Do NOT open load valves rapidly **
  ** Pressure MUST rise gradually (0-5 bar within 1 minute) **
  
  Instructions:
    1. Monitor discharge pressure gauge continuously
    2. Using load control valve on panel, open discharge SLOWLY
       Action: Turn load valve knob counterclockwise by 1/4 turn increments
       Timing: Wait 10 seconds between each 1/4 turn
    3. Observe pressure gauge rise in stages:
       Expected timeline:
       - 0-10 seconds: 0-2 bar (pump finding load)
       - 10-30 seconds: 2-5 bar (gradual buildup)
       - 30-60 seconds: 5-15 bar (normal operating pressure approaching)
       - 60-90 seconds: 15-20 bar (final pressure stabilization)
    
    4. CAVITATION DETECTION during pressure buildup:
       Listen for abnormal sounds:
       - NORMAL: Quiet hydraulic whine (smooth, consistent tone)
       - CAVITATION: Grinding or crackling noise (STOP immediately if heard)
       - ACTION if cavitation detected:
         a) Press STOP button to shutdown pump
         b) Wait 2 minutes for pressure to drop to zero
         c) Check inlet pressure gauge: Should show 2.0-2.5 bar (suction available)
         d) If inlet pressure <1.0 bar: INLET BLOCKAGE SUSPECTED
            - Notify maintenance supervisor - do NOT restart
         e) If inlet pressure normal: Restart procedure at STEP 5

    5. Pressure gauge reading at 2 minutes:
       Expected: 18-20 bar (normal operating pressure)
       Acceptable range: 15-25 bar
       If above 25 bar: Reduce load valve opening slightly
       If below 15 bar: Check that load valve opened fully

□ STEP 7: SYSTEM STABILIZATION CHECK
  Time: 3 minutes
  
  Instructions:
    1. Monitor all parameters for 3 minutes at operating pressure (18-20 bar):
       Parameters to observe:
       
       A) Discharge Pressure Gauge:
          Expected: Steady reading (±0.5 bar fluctuation acceptable)
          Alarm condition: Pressure oscillating >2 bar (indicates blockage or cavitation)
          Action: STOP if pressure unstable
       
       B) Inlet Pressure Gauge (NEW - added post-Jan 2024):
          Expected: 2.0-2.5 bar (suction available)
          Alarm condition: Inlet pressure <1.0 bar (STOP immediately)
          Alarm condition: Inlet pressure dropping (indicates strainer blockage developing)
          Action: Monitor trend - if dropping, stop and check strainer
       
       C) Flow Rate Display (if equipped):
          Expected: 145-150 L/min (nominal rated flow)
          Acceptable range: 130-155 L/min
          Alarm condition: <130 L/min (indicates system blockage)
       
       D) Temperature Gauge:
          Expected: Temperature rising gradually from ambient to 50-55°C (takes 10-15 min)
          Alarm condition: Temperature rising rapidly (>1°C per minute)
          Alarm condition: Temperature exceeding 60°C during startup
          Action: Check cooler operation - cooler fan should be running by now
       
       E) Acoustic Check:
          Expected: Steady hydraulic whine, no grinding or crackling
          Unacceptable: Any unusual noise (stop and diagnose)
       
       F) Vibration Check:
          Expected: Minimal vibration (hold hand on pump body - slight tremor only)
          Alarm condition: Severe vibration (>7 mm/s) - indicates bearing issue
          Action: STOP if excessive vibration detected

    2. RECORD STARTUP DATA:
       In startup logbook, record at 3-minute mark:
       - Start time: __ : __ (HH:MM)
       - Operator name: _________________
       - Discharge pressure: _____ bar
       - Inlet pressure: _____ bar
       - Flow rate: _____ L/min
       - Temperature: _____ °C
       - Cooler fan: ON / OFF (check visually)
       - Abnormal observations: _________________________

OPERATIONAL MONITORING PHASE

□ STEP 8: CONTINUOUS MONITORING (First 30 minutes of operation)
  Time: 30 minutes
  Frequency: Check parameters every 5 minutes
  
  Actions:
    1. Every 5 minutes, verify:
       - Discharge pressure gauge (steady, not fluctuating)
       - Inlet pressure gauge (should remain 2.0-2.5 bar)
       - Temperature rise (normal: +0.5°C per minute, reaching 50-55°C by 15-min mark)
       - Noise level (consistent, no grinding)
       - No visible leaks developing
    
    2. Temperature ramp during first 30 minutes:
       Minute 5: ~40°C (warm-up starting)
       Minute 10: ~45°C (approaching normal range)
       Minute 15: ~50°C (normal range reached)
       Minute 20-30: ~50-55°C (stabilized at normal operating temperature)
       
       If temperature exceeds 58°C by minute 20:
         - Increase cooler fan speed (if variable speed cooler installed)
         - Check that discharge cooler outlet has good flow (no blockage)
         - Reduce pump load slightly (1/4 turn on load valve counterclockwise)
    
    3. Pressure stability check:
       Discharge pressure should remain within ±1 bar of operating setpoint
       If pressure fluctuates >2 bar: Check inlet pressure immediately
       - Inlet pressure dropping? = Strainer blockage suspected → STOP
       - Inlet pressure stable, discharge fluctuating? = Load side blockage → Investigate
    
    4. Seal leakage observation:
       Check pump body drain line (small rubber hose on pump side)
       Expected: Occasional drops, <5 drops per minute
       Alarm: Continuous stream of fluid = Seal failure → STOP and notify maintenance

□ STEP 9: FULL OPERATIONAL STATUS (After 30 minutes)
  Time: 1 minute
  Actions:
    1. Verify all parameters are within normal operating range:
       ✓ Discharge pressure: 18-20 bar (stable)
       ✓ Inlet pressure: 2.0-2.5 bar (stable)
       ✓ Temperature: 50-55°C (stabilized)
       ✓ Flow rate: 145-150 L/min
       ✓ Noise: Consistent, no abnormal sounds
       ✓ Vibration: Normal, <6 mm/s
       ✓ No visible leaks
    
    2. System is now FULLY OPERATIONAL and ready for normal production use

SHUTDOWN SEQUENCE - NORMAL OPERATION
================================================================================

SHUTDOWN PROCEDURE (at end of shift or scheduled maintenance)
Time Allocated: 5 minutes
Equipment: All shutdown controls must be manually operated

□ STEP A: PRESSURE REDUCTION (Controlled discharge)
  Time: 1 minute
  
  Instructions:
    1. Turn load control valve clockwise (reducing outlet opening)
       Action: 1/4 turn every 5 seconds until valve is fully closed
       Timing: Takes approximately 20-30 seconds to fully close
    2. Monitor discharge pressure gauge:
       Expected: Pressure drops gradually to zero over 30 seconds
       DO NOT: Close discharge ball valve (let load control reduce pressure naturally)

□ STEP B: MOTOR SHUTDOWN
  Time: 1 minute
  
  Instructions:
    1. Press green STOP button on control panel (held position, no twist needed)
    2. Listen for motor wind-down sound (should stop within 3-5 seconds)
    3. Observe pressure gauge needle returns to zero
    4. Turn main electrical breaker to OFF (lever down)
    5. Confirm green power light extinguishes

□ STEP C: POST-SHUTDOWN CHECKS
  Time: 3 minutes
  
  Instructions:
    1. Wait 2 minutes before approaching pump (allow fluid to cool slightly)
    2. Visual inspection of pump body:
       - Check for any new leaks that developed during operation
       - Observe drain line: Normal if 1-2 drops visible (allow 30 sec observation)
    3. Check reservoir fluid level (should still be between MIN and MAX)
    4. Verify isolation ball valves remain OPEN (leave system pressurized for next startup)
    5. Record shutdown time in logbook:
       - Shutdown time: __ : __ (HH:MM)
       - Total operating time: _____ hours
       - Any issues encountered: _______________________
       - Next scheduled maintenance: ___________________

EMERGENCY SHUTDOWN (If abnormality detected)
Actions:
  1. Immediately press RED emergency stop button
  2. DO NOT attempt to correct problem while pump is running
  3. Wait 3 minutes for complete pump shutdown
  4. Contact maintenance supervisor before restarting
  5. Do NOT restart until root cause is identified and corrected

TROUBLESHOOTING QUICK REFERENCE
================================================================================

PROBLEM: Pump will not start (motor does not hum after START button pressed)
  Cause 1: Main electrical breaker is OFF
  Solution: Turn main breaker to ON position, retry START
  
  Cause 2: Emergency stop button is pressed in (red button depressed)
  Solution: Turn emergency stop button counterclockwise to RESET position
  
  Cause 3: Motor circuit breaker has tripped (safety cutout)
  Solution: Find circuit breaker panel, reset breaker, retry START
    Note: If breaker trips repeatedly, do NOT restart - call maintenance

PROBLEM: Pump starts but no pressure builds (gauge stays at 0 bar)
  Cause 1: Discharge ball valve is CLOSED
  Solution: Locate discharge ball valve (near outlet), turn handle parallel to pipe
  
  Cause 2: Inlet ball valve is CLOSED
  Solution: Locate inlet ball valve (near inlet), turn handle parallel to pipe
  
  Cause 3: Pump cavitating (inlet blockage)
  Solution: STOP pump. Check inlet pressure gauge - if <1.0 bar, strainer is blocked.
    Contact maintenance supervisor for strainer cleaning

PROBLEM: Pressure builds rapidly to >25 bar (relief opening)
  Cause: System overload or relief valve setting incorrect
  Solution: Reduce load using load control valve (turn counterclockwise)
    If pressure remains high: STOP pump and notify maintenance

PROBLEM: Audible cavitation sound (grinding/crackling) during startup
  Cause 1: Inlet strainer blockage
  Solution: STOP pump. Contact maintenance for strainer inspection/cleaning
  
  Cause 2: Inlet pressure too low (<1.0 bar)
  Solution: Check reservoir fluid level - may need top-up before restarting

PROBLEM: Temperature rises rapidly (>2°C per minute) or exceeds 60°C during startup
  Cause: Cooler not functioning or flow restricted
  Solution: Verify cooler fan is running (visual check on cooler unit)
    If fan not running: Check fan motor power, contact maintenance
    Reduce pump load temporarily until cooler brings temp down to 55°C

PROBLEM: Visible fluid leak developing during operation
  Cause 1: Minor seal weeping (1-5 drops per minute from drain)
  Solution: This is normal - monitor only, no action required
  
  Cause 2: Major leak (continuous stream from drain line)
  Solution: STOP pump immediately. Do NOT restart. Call maintenance supervisor.

PROBLEM: Excessive vibration or noise during operation
  Cause: Bearing wear or impeller cavitation damage
  Solution: STOP pump immediately. Do NOT continue operation.
    Contact maintenance supervisor for bearing/seal inspection
