INCIDENT OVERVIEW
Incident Type: Cavitation event detected during normal operation
Equipment Affected: PUMP-XYZ (Grundfos SPM 150-11)
Location: Steel Plant, Unit A - Coolant Circulation System
Date of Incident: 2024-01-11 (10:32 AM IST)
Time Duration: ~45 minutes (detected to resolution)
Severity: MODERATE (Non-critical, equipment protected, no personnel injury)
Downtime: 1 hour (incident detection + corrective action + verification)

INCIDENT DETECTION
Symptom Description: Operators reported audible cavitation noise from PUMP-XYZ pump body
Noise Characteristics: "Grinding/crackling sound" starting at 10:32 AM
Operator: Harish Prabhu (Shift Supervisor)
Initial Response Time: 4 minutes (alarm escalation procedure followed)
Supporting Evidence: Pressure monitoring data shows inlet pressure oscillation 0.2-0.8 bar (abnormal)

PRESSURE ANALYSIS DATA
Normal Operating Baseline (Pre-incident):
  - Discharge pressure: 19.2 bar (constant)
  - Inlet pressure: 2.1-2.3 bar (suction gauge)
  - Pressure differential: 16.9-17.1 bar
  - Operating conditions: Stable, zero fluctuation

Abnormal Readings During Incident (10:32 AM - 11:17 AM):
  - Discharge pressure: 8.2-18.9 bar (extreme fluctuation, cycling 5-6 times/minute)
  - Inlet pressure: 0.2-0.8 bar (severe suction side instability)
  - Pressure differential: Unstable, indicating vapor bubble formation
  - System temperature: Rose from 54°C to 62°C in 10 minutes
  - Flow rate: Dropped to 85 L/min (from nominal 150 L/min)

Root Cause Analysis
Primary Root Cause: Inlet Strainer Blockage (Confirmed)
Evidence:
  1. Post-incident disassembly revealed 40-micron strainer cartridge 80% occluded
  2. Particulate analysis: Fine rust/oxidation debris, iron oxide particles (0.5-40 micron range)
  3. Strainer pressure drop across element: Estimated >2.0 bar (calculated from inlet pressure drop)
  4. Visual inspection confirmed debris accumulation pattern (not isolated spots)

Root Cause Timeline:
  - Last strainer replacement: 2023-10-11 (during cavitation response, EVENT 3)
  - Operational hours since last replacement: 1,247 hours
  - Expected life of cartridge: 1,500 hours minimum (per OEM Grundfos specifications)
  - Actual service life observed: 83% of expected life at blockage point
  - Probable origin: Rust from exposed carbon steel surfaces in hot return line (100°C bulk oil temp)

Contributing Factor: Elevated bulk oil temperature (58-62°C observed)
  - Accelerates oxidation rate of hydraulic fluid, increasing particulate generation
  - Thermal degradation of oil seals increases shedding
  - Temperature control system under review (see Preventive Actions)

Secondary Contributing Factor: Maintenance interval not adjusted for hot environment
  - Steel plant ambient: 45-50°C (high sensible heat)
  - Coolant return temperature: 55-60°C (design spec 40-55°C)
  - No preventive shortening of strainer change interval implemented

CAVITATION MECHANISM EXPLAINED
Cavitation Onset Condition Met:
  - Inlet absolute pressure dropped below fluid vapor pressure (0.3-0.4 bar @ 58°C)
  - Vapor bubbles formed within pump impeller suction region
  - Bubble collapse induced pressure spikes and erosive shock waves
  
Physical Damage Assessment:
  - Impeller inlet vane edges: Micro-pitting visible (erosion depth: <0.1 mm - minor)
  - Pump housing interior: No significant erosion (protected by ductile iron hardness)
  - Shaft deflection: Measurable runout increase to 0.08 mm (within tolerance, but elevated)
  - Mechanical seals (NBR + FKM): NO DAMAGE (pressure-balanced design protected seals)
  - Bearing clearance: Unchanged, inspected post-incident (SKF 6205-2RS - good condition)

Risk Assessment of Continuing Cavitation:
  - Had incident persisted >2 hours: Impeller fatigue failure probable
  - Mechanical seal damage risk: Low due to balanced cavity design
  - Shaft damage risk: Moderate (11% increase in runout over 45 minutes suggests rapid erosion)
  - Overall equipment risk: MODERATE if left unattended

RESOLUTION & CORRECTIVE ACTION
Immediate Actions Taken (10:32 AM - 11:30 AM):
  1. 10:40 AM - Pump shutdown initiated by operator (normal shut-down sequence followed)
  2. 10:50 AM - Technician Vikram Patel (ID: TECH-028) arrived on-site
  3. 11:05 AM - Inlet strainer cartridge removed and visual inspection performed
  4. 11:10 AM - Cartridge element cleaned (compressed air at 4 bar, 5 minute duration)
  5. 11:15 AM - Cleaned cartridge reinstalled, system flushed at low pressure (5 bar, 15 minutes)
  6. 11:20 AM - Pump restart with gradual pressure ramp (0-5 bar over 2 minutes)
  7. 11:30 AM - Full operational pressure achieved (19.5 bar), cavitation ceased immediately
  8. 11:35 AM - Post-start verification: Inlet pressure 2.2 bar, discharge stable, noise normal

Cartridge Cleaning Procedure:
  - Method: Reverse flow flushing with 40-micron bypass strainer
  - Flushing duration: 30 liters of ISO VG 32 hydraulic oil circulated
  - Post-cleaning pressure drop across element: 0.15 bar (normal, <0.5 bar specification)
  - Particulate captured in bypass strainer analyzed: Iron oxide dominant (rust oxidation)

System Verification Post-Resolution (11:35 AM - 1:00 PM):
  - Pressure stability test: 5 hours continuous operation at 20 bar - PASS
  - Vibration monitoring: 5.8 mm/s (excellent, no abnormal resonance)
  - Temperature monitoring: Peak 56°C (return to normal operating range)
  - Seal inspection: No leakage observed, both NBR and FKM seals intact
  - Flow rate: 148 L/min (98.7% of nominal - minor reduction acceptable)
  - Noise level: 76 dB(A) (normal, cavitation noise completely eliminated)

PREVENTIVE ACTIONS & RECOMMENDATIONS
Action 1: Strainer Cartridge Replacement Schedule Acceleration
  - Previous interval: 1,500 operating hours (per OEM baseline)
  - New interval: 1,000 operating hours (33% reduction)
  - Rationale: Steel plant thermal environment accelerates oil degradation
  - Responsible party: Maintenance supervisor
  - Target completion: Implement by 2024-02-01
  - Cost impact: +INR 800 per cycle (additional cartridge per year, estimated)

Action 2: Bulk Oil Temperature Control Enhancement
  - Install thermostatic valve on return line (set point: 50°C)
  - Upgrade cooler capacity from 15 kW to 20 kW (HYDAC FC3100 recommended)
  - Add temperature alarm at 58°C (manual operator alert)
  - Target completion: 2024-03-15
  - Cost estimate: INR 35,000 (cooler upgrade) + INR 8,000 (thermostat valve + installation)

Action 3: Maintenance Procedure Documentation Update
  - Create work instruction: "Hot Environment Strainer Maintenance"
  - Include trigger: Automatic strainer change when inlet pressure rises >0.5 bar above baseline
  - Add alarm limits: Suction pressure <1.2 bar triggers mandatory inspection
  - Documentation owner: Plant engineering manager
  - Target completion: 2024-02-15

Action 4: Operator Training Enhancement
  - Topic: Recognition of cavitation symptoms (audible, pressure fluctuation, temperature rise)
  - Duration: 30-minute module
  - Training completion target: All shift supervisors by 2024-02-28
  - Trainer: Senior technician Amit Sharma (ID: TECH-012)
  - Assessment: Each operator must demonstrate correct shutdown and escalation procedure

Action 5: Equipment Monitoring System Upgrade
  - Install continuous inlet pressure transmitter (0-10 bar range) with 4-20 mA output
  - Connect to Plant DCS (Distributed Control System) for real-time monitoring
  - Set alarm threshold: Inlet pressure <1.0 bar (early cavitation warning)
  - Target completion: 2024-03-30
  - Cost estimate: INR 18,000 (transducer, cable, DCS integration)

Action 6: Post-Incident Monitoring Schedule
  - Daily pressure checks: 2024-01-11 to 2024-01-25 (14 days post-incident)
  - Weekly checks: 2024-01-26 to 2024-02-29 (1 month ongoing)
  - Monthly checks: From 2024-03-01 onward (return to normal schedule)
  - Data logging: Record inlet pressure, discharge pressure, temperature, and flow each check

LESSONS LEARNED
1. Thermal environment significantly impacts equipment maintenance intervals (50% reduction applied)
2. Cavitation can develop rapidly (45 minutes from detection to critical condition) but is reversible if caught early
3. Operator training and symptom recognition critical to equipment protection
4. Mechanical seal design (pressure-balanced) proved effective in protecting seals during cavitation event
5. Preventive strainer maintenance has direct ROI in equipment protection (cost prevention >INR 50,000 for impeller replacement)
